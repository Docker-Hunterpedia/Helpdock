How an agent’s reply looks on WhatsApp, Messenger and Instagram

Neutral frames, not the platforms’ own look. The customer’s messages sit at the inline end, the business’s at the inline start. A reply is plain text, so names and references are never parsed, and it leaves as several messages: the text, cut at the platform’s limit, then each attachment on its own. Numbers match the notes below; dashed labels are notes on the board, not text the customer sees.

Message
  1. 1
    First line: who is writing (M10-06)An agent’s reply starts with their first name and the brand, since the number, Page or account has one name. Only the first message of a reply carries it.
  2. 2
    A long reply in partsText over the limit is cut at a paragraph, else a sentence, else a word: WhatsApp 4096 characters, Messenger 2000, Instagram 1000 bytes. Parts go out in order with no “1/2” marks; on WhatsApp Helpdock waits six seconds between messages to one customer, as Meta asks.
  3. 3
    Each attachment on its ownAfter the text, every attachment is a message of its own. On WhatsApp an image or a video carries its file name as the caption; Messenger and Instagram show no caption.
  4. 4
    Documents keep their file nameWhatsApp shows a document with its file name, type and size. Messenger sends other files as files; Instagram takes PDF files only.
  5. 5
    A voice note as audioAn audio attachment goes out as audio: the platform draws a player with its length and no caption.
  6. 6
    A file Instagram refusesInstagram sends PDF files and images up to 8 MB. Any other file is left out of the reply and the customer gets this line instead, in their language; the agent sees “left out” under the reply.
  7. 7
    Instagram counts bytes1000 bytes is about 1000 English characters but only about 500 Arabic letters, which take two bytes each. Meta/Chat-AR shows an Arabic reply cut into two parts.
  8. 8
    Nothing else is sent in M10No welcome, no “ticket opened” message, no automatic reply and no satisfaction survey on these channels: only what an agent writes. The ticks are the platform’s own; Helpdock reads them as Delivered and Read.
